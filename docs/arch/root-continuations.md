# Pollable root computations and dependency handoffs

A host root computation (component metadata, compile) must be able to stop
when it needs work it does not own. A poll returns `Pending` with the
computation's state owned and suspended, and a race-safe wake subscription
registered. A later poll continues from that point, on any thread, without
redoing completed work. This page is the reference for the host roots and
the boundaries that already behave this way. It also lists, as an inventory
of the current tree, the boundaries a root can still block on.

## Current delivery boundary

The public roots provide owned `Send` state, exclusive polling, per-poll
ambient restoration and an external work handoff. They are only partially
resumable. A caller must not assume that every poll, or every handed-out
producer, returns before an incomplete nested dependency settles.

| Delivered and exercised | Remaining conversion or proof |
| --- | --- |
| Store-view and per-attempt snapshot capture, owner indexing and overlay indexing suspend through their existing handoffs | Metadata computation, extraction/fallthrough and output materialization still call synchronous dependency paths |
| Cache admission, scheduler completion, resolver flights, carrier publication/stable parse, store-view capture, lowering and workspace resolution offer task-owned handoffs with independent cancellation | Higher callers must consume these owned APIs throughout their traversal instead of driving compatibility entries inside a poll |
| Root state and converted scopes move between polling threads | Nested semantic dispatch still drives tasks or waits on subscriptions synchronously; complete-root no-replay is not proved |
| Compile roots hand out one macro producer and retain followers, which take its bundle only when the producer's observations hold in their own view and otherwise produce their own; their blocker routes, blocker loads and dependency-verdict walk suspend through the owned resolution and load handoffs, and a stopped load or request answers its typed stop | That producer runs a synchronous traversal; taking a route can still ingest configured programs inside the poll for an importer no configured project lists |
| An external consumer fixture and a queued-wake driver exercise multiple roots | The driver does not yet prove progress across every nested family with admission closed and no retained synchronous stack |

The parse-once, zero eager-body and one shallow-walk assertions cover overlay
input capture. They do not establish those properties across a complete
metadata or compile root. Likewise, the private-driver compile-fail fixture
denies direct access to `drive`; it does not seal off transitive blocking
adapters. The remaining conversion must retain the same semantic answers,
generated bytes, diagnostics, receipts and charge ownership.

## Protocol shared by every pollable boundary

Every boundary listed under [Delivered boundaries](#delivered-boundaries)
follows the same rules:

- **Check and register atomically.** A poll checks readiness and, while the
  work is not ready, registers the poll's `Waker` inside the same critical
  section the producer takes to publish. A completion that lands between the
  check and the registration therefore still wakes the poller.
- **Wake after unlock.** A producer takes the registered wakers under its
  lock and wakes them only after releasing it. No wake runs under a
  producer, queue, flight or publication lock. Nested cancellation-owner
  probes latch discovered cancellations immediately and defer their callbacks
  until the enclosing owner and subscription locks have all released.
- **One registration per subscriber.** Re-polling with a waker that wakes the
  same task keeps the registration. Any other waker replaces it. Dropping
  the subscriber withdraws exactly its own registration and leaves a
  sibling's alone, even when the sibling registered the same executor waker.
- **A wake only schedules.** A wake means a later poll will make progress.
  It never runs the waiting computation, so a readiness callback cannot
  re-enter a driver.
- **A driver subscribes before checking progress.** A synchronous root driver
  registers its `SchedulerProgressSubscription` before polling the root and
  running a dispatch step. Returning the final shared capacity permit or
  making a dependent DAG node ready wakes that interest after lifecycle
  unlock and posts an inbox wake for the native driver. A release racing the
  readiness check therefore cannot strand queued work until an idle repump.
  Cooperative dispatch carries the worker's active path, preserving capacity
  loans for nested dependencies without running a producer inside polling.
- **Owned, `Send`, one poller.** Subscribers own their state and hold no
  borrow of the producer. They are `Send` whenever their result is, so a
  suspended subscriber may resume on another thread. One poller drives a
  subscriber at a time (`poll` takes `&mut self`).
- **Owned cancellation and task waits.** Owned dependency adapters register
  the stop token and the waiter-to-producer edge in the shared registry.
  Cancellation wakes and detaches that subscriber without stopping siblings.
  Raw readiness subscriptions, including `SlotJoin` and
  `CompletionSubscription`, acquire this ownership through their owned
  adapters; readiness alone does not provide cancellation or a task edge.

## Host roots

A host root is one public request as an owned computation. It is begun
without running any of it, and an external driver polls it to its answer.

| Entry | Root | Answer | Synchronous entry over it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `VerterHost::begin_component_meta_output(canonical_or_alias)` | `ComponentMetaOutputRoot<'h>` | `ComponentMetaOutputAnswer` | `get_component_meta_output` |
| `VerterHost::begin_compile_request(canonical_id, request)` | `CompileRequestRoot<'h>` | `CompileRequestAnswer` | `compile_request` |

All of these are in `crates/verter_session/src/root_continuation.rs` and
re-exported from the crate root with `RootWork`.

- **Ownership and lifetime.** A root borrows only the host (`'h`). It owns
  its request, every input it captures (the session view, the store-view
  capture, the compile input) and the suspended state of the wait it is in,
  so it outlives the call that began it. It is `Send`, and `poll` takes it
  exclusively, so a pending root may resume on any thread, one poller at a
  time. The session crate's tests assert the `Send` bound.
- **Ambient request state.** A root runs under the request context and the
  scheduler job its creator ran under, captured when it begins, and as one
  execution task of the host's `TaskRegistry`: the task its creator runs as,
  or one registered for it. Each poll installs the three on the polling
  thread and restores the thread's own afterwards; a root begun outside any
  request clears the poller's request slots for the poll instead. The task
  owns the converted semantic, scoped-cache and resolver producers and is
  the waiter of their task-registered flights. Their same-path recursion and
  wait cycles are decided by task across threads. The other dependency
  families expose the same ownership through their owned adapters; higher
  callers still need to carry those adapters through traversal. Dropping a
  pending root releases its suspended state under the same ambient state.
- **Result and errors.** `Ready` carries exactly what the synchronous entry
  returns, including its typed failures. Polling after `Ready` panics.
- **Dependency work.** At a converted handoff, a root transfers the
  dependency's producer or scheduler dispatch step instead of running it
  inside its poll. The poll returns `Pending` and leaves one
  `RootWork` for the driver (`take_work`). The driver runs it wherever it
  chooses — `RootWork::is_producer` separates CPU work from a scheduler
  dispatch step — and polls again; `RootWork::run` answers whether the work
  made progress (a dispatch step that dispatched nothing returns `false`,
  and the dependency's own settlement then wakes the root). Nothing else
  wakes a root that handed out work. The work is itself pollable:
  `RootWork::poll(&Waker)` advances it one step on any thread and answers
  `Ready(progressed)` when it is done, and `run` is that poll driven to its
  end on the calling thread. Today's producer body has no wait of its own,
  so its first poll runs it and answers `Ready(true)`; polling work after
  it answered panics. Dropping a transferred producer abandons
  its dependency, and the next poll answers that abandonment. Roots that
  followed the dropped producer are not failed by it (see the compile
  request below). Dropping a
  dispatch opportunity leaves the admitted dependency live; a later poll may
  offer another dispatch step or producer, or remain pending. Dropping the
  root withdraws its subscription and request ownership.
- **Backpressure.** A root waits on admission like any other pending
  dependency; it adds no queue of its own.
- **External driving.** The synchronous entry drives the same root with
  `verter_execution::blocking::block_on(WaitFamily::Root, ..)` on its own
  thread, running handed-out producers where the scheduler runs producers
  (`Scheduler::run_transferred_producer`: its CPU pool natively, the caller
  on wasm32) and dispatch steps on its own thread
  (`Scheduler::dispatch_for_pending_dependency`). This is the compatibility
  driver's pool binding for transferred work. Unconverted nested paths still
  reach the blocking boundaries inventoried below.

What each root suspends on today:

- **Component-metadata output.** The root runs as a request of its own when
  it is begun outside one: it creates that request's budgeted context where
  it begins and carries it as its ambient request, so every poll runs under
  the same request. The store-view capture it starts with suspends behind a
  build another thread holds (see [Store-view reads](#store-view-reads)). A
  cold component's resolve is pollable through the capture of its inputs:
  the stable-request driver (`run_stable_request_pollable`), the request
  host's `capture_component_meta_inputs` (an `async` trait method), the
  raw analysis snapshot with its imports' resolution, and the owner's
  IndexedReady build, so the root suspends on the owner's declaration-
  lowering shard instead of parking there. The compute that resolve runs
  in (`traced_component_meta_compute_pollable`) and its dispatcher
  (`ProjectSemanticDispatch::suspendable`) cross that suspension with it
  ([Scopes a suspended compute carries](#scopes-a-suspended-compute-carries)).
  The resolution's own semantic evaluation, extraction and output
  materialization still enter their dependency waits synchronously inside
  the poll; see [Boundaries that still block](#boundaries-that-still-block).
- **Compile request.** The macro bundle it compiles with is produced on a
  pollable path: the store-view read suspends; the scoped cache node
  suspends while another root produces it and while it waits for dispatch
  (handing out a dispatch step); and when the node elects this root its
  producer, the root hands the producer out instead of running it. The
  backend and its compile arena are created after the bundle is produced,
  so neither is held across a suspension, and parse-stack refusals are
  recorded across polls (`refusals_within_pollable`). The macro traversal
  inside the handed-out producer runs synchronously wherever the driver
  runs it; its own dependency waits are the semantic engine's.

  A root that followed another caller's producer does not take its bundle
  unchecked (`delivery_of` in `typeinfo/vue_macro_codegen.rs`, shared with
  the synchronous macro bundle entry). It takes the bundle when every
  observation the producer recorded holds in the root's own view, with the
  owner as its strict self-root (`MacroFactFootprint::holds_under`, the
  validator a warm semantic-memo hit uses); a footprint that recorded
  nothing vouches for nothing. An absence is not a recorded fact, so a
  bundle that reports a dependency failure is not taken on its first
  delivery. In either case the root begins the scoped node again over its
  own seed and overlay: the settled flight is gone, so the roots that begin
  it again share one new flight and elect one producer among themselves.
  A root whose own driver dropped its producer answers
  `Host(HostError::Cancelled)`, the abandonment above; a root that followed
  that producer and whose own request is live begins the node again and
  completes. A producer's supersession still reaches its followers as
  `Superseded`: they share its lane, whose pin folds every external
  dimension the supersession could have moved, so a follower that produced
  again would abort the same way.

  Before it captures its input, the compile root hydrates its blockers
  (`host_resolve/compile_blocker_hydration.rs`): it resolves each external
  block source and macro type import, records the owner's dependency edges
  and loads each resolved blocker through `begin_load`. Each route
  resolves through `VerterHost::resolve_for_persistent_state_pollable`,
  the pollable twin of the synchronous `resolve_for_persistent_state`:
  both take the importer's route (`import_resolution_route`: the importer
  mapping, configured-program ingestion and ownership choice), record the
  owner edge (`observed_resolution`) and admit the answer with its witness
  (`admit_persistent_resolution`); the twin resolves on that route through
  the workspace's owned handoff (`begin_resolution` with the route's
  owner). While a resolution is pending the root hands out the
  resolution's producer and returns `Pending`; while a load is pending it
  hands out a scheduler dispatch step. A workspace without the owned
  protocol answers in place. Taking the route can still ingest configured
  programs inside the poll for an importer no configured project lists.
  Hydration's outcome is typed
  (`BlockerHydration`): a refused route or a load that failed is a gap and
  the remaining blockers still load. A load stopped by cancellation,
  supersession or shutdown ends hydration with that abort before the
  capture, and the request answers it as
  `CompileRequestFailure::Host(Cancelled | Superseded | Shutdown)`: nothing
  is captured or compiled, no blocker source is integrated after the stop,
  and no flight is left. The dependency edges and resolution witnesses
  recorded for routes that resolved before the stop stay, as any later
  request would record them again. The request's cancellation reaches a
  resolving route or loading blocker through a subscription on the
  request's own token, which the computation polls beside each handoff, so
  the cancellation itself wakes the root; the load keeps the host-wide
  token as its own stop. A root begun outside a request has no request
  token and stops only on shutdown, reset, the host-wide token or being
  dropped. The capture
  (`capture_compile_input`) takes that outcome by value, so it cannot run
  before hydration. Input preparation (`prepare_execution_inputs_pollable`
  in `host_resolve/compile_inputs.rs`) then produces the macro bundle and
  decides the dependency verdict before the bound backend runs; the output
  side receives only complete inputs. A missing macro type dependency the
  host could not establish (the payload lookup ended early, or a route or
  file on the way to it is a known gap) answers
  `CompileRequestFailure::DependenciesIncomplete` with those gaps and the
  same diagnostics a proved-absent dependency reports; a proved-absent one
  stays `Refused`. On that failure path the verdict's walk to the export
  (`host_resolve/vue_macro_dependency_diagnostics.rs`) reads each file
  along the route through `ensure_indexed_ready_serve_owning_loads`,
  handing out a dispatch step while the read is pending, and resolves each
  step through the same pollable twin, so it too enters no wait inside the
  poll. The profile-derived lanes (the virtual-file lane and the runtime
  render lane) run the same hydration through its synchronous entry
  (`hydrate_compile_blockers`), which resolves each route with
  `resolve_for_persistent_state` and loads each blocker in place with
  `ensure_loaded` as before, and run the same verdict walk in place
  (`collect`), and keep their diagnostics. They take their input through the same
  `capture_compile_input`, handing it the outcome of their own hydration;
  no lane assembles a compile input of its own. The capture also returns
  the block-content stamp a publishing lane fences its publication on,
  and mints the source witness only when the lane asks for it.

## Execution tasks across suspension

Producers and waits are owned by execution tasks, never by threads, wherever
a computation can suspend while it holds them:

- **Semantic memo flights and scoped cache nodes** register task wait edges
  in the host's one `TaskRegistry` (below).
- **Resolver request flights** (`SingleflightGroup`, used by stable
  requests, fallthrough requests, imported roots and IndexedReady
  materialization) take the host registry at construction. The shared core
  retains the producer's execution task. `run_retaining_pollable` lets its
  leader suspend inside the compute under the polling root's task;
  `run_retaining` externally drives a distinct synchronous producer task.
  Compatibility reentry or a wait cycle computes inline as a nested leader;
  owned callers receive a typed cycle refusal. Other tasks register their
  `follower -> leader` edge before following. Dropping a suspended leader
  aborts the lane: compatibility followers re-elect and owned followers
  receive the typed abandonment. Dropping a follower withdraws its
  registration and edge. Evidence:
  `resolver_core::singleflight_task_tests` (two tasks on one thread share
  one leader; re-entry and cycles compute inline; dropped leaders and
  followers release), each failed by an applied thread-style or edge-less
  mutation.
- **Workspace resolution flights** retain a producer task in an owned
  `FlightLease`. Each follower has its own subscription and task wait edge;
  same-path and mixed-family cycles return `Cycle`. Producer leases and
  resolution work can move across threads without relying on a thread-local
  lease count. See [Workspace resolution](#workspace-resolution).
- **Store-view build claims** remain owned by their independent producer
  through external snapshot or reclamation work. Subscribers may suspend
  while that claim is held; reset retains a physically running claim until
  release. See [Base store-view capture](#base-store-view-capture).

## Scopes a suspended compute carries

A scope a computation opens on its thread and holds across a suspension is
owned by the computation instead, and each of its polls installs it on the
polling thread for that poll alone. Nothing of it stays on a thread between
polls, so the computation may resume anywhere, and a synchronous caller —
one poll that runs to the end — sees the scope exactly as it was before.

| Scope | Owned as | Installed per poll by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request context and budget | the root's ambient request, created where it begins when none is current | `RootTask` |
| Component-meta spend log | `request_budget::ComputeSpendLog` | `traced_component_meta_compute_pollable` |
| Component-meta span | `connected_demand::ComputeSpan` | the same |
| Fact tracer | `OwnedFactTracer::fanning_out` (observations reach the scopes below it on the polling thread, as a synchronous tracer's do) | the same |
| Request phase audit (`semantic-observe`) | `component_meta_audit::RequestAudit` | `within_request_audit` |
| Construction lane | `SuspendableDispatch`: a dispatcher bound to no thread, whose lane is entered for each poll (a lane owns nothing, so no operation of it may span a suspension) | `SuspendableDispatch::run` |
| Work attribution | the site | `attribute_polls` |
| Parse-stack refusals | the recording | `refusals_within_pollable` |
| Demand scope of a helper's requested read | `query_frames::DemandScope` on the frame that produces the read, inherited by the frames its needs start | `step_instantiate_frame` (each step) |

Each converted computation has one implementation: the pollable form. Its
synchronous form drives it — `drive_pending` under the caller's execution
task where it may wait, `complete_now` where every step runs in place and
the first poll must answer. A driver takes its computation pinned where the
caller builds it, so the state is not copied into the driver's frame, and
the cold component-meta path builds its large nested computations with
`on_heap`, from a frame that ends before they run, so a synchronous caller
does not hold their state on its stack while the compute recurses below
them.

## Interior state a suspended computation shares

`ProjectSemanticDispatch` keeps its request state in
`verter_execution::sync_cell`. `SyncRefCell` preserves `RefCell` borrow
rules: conflicting borrows panic instead of waiting, and its guards are
not `Send`, so a borrow cannot span a suspension. `SyncCell<T>` stores an
arbitrary `Copy` value under a mutex. `SyncBool`, `SyncUsize`, `SyncU16`,
`SyncU32` and `SyncU64` use the corresponding typed atomics where the target
supports that width, falling back to `SyncCell` otherwise. Reads acquire, writes
release and replacement acquires and releases; wide values are always
read and replaced whole. No generic value is reinterpreted as atomic bytes.

The connected-demand ledger uses scalar cells for its flags, work and byte
counts, limits and query depth. Refusal reasons use their explicit `u32`
bit encoding; request-effect operation and paid counts use typed scalar cells.
Exclusive receipt totals accumulate in scalar cells and flush to the recording
at scope and prefix transitions; budget checks still run at every charge.
Pending totals move with the ledger during a task handoff. The dispatcher's
operation depth, degraded-read counter and evidence/budget epochs use the same
typed scalar cells. The dispatcher is `Sync`, allowing a `Send`
computation to resume on another thread, but each computation still has one
driver: compound updates and two-cell swaps are not atomic transactions.
Cancellation, sticky refusal and logical charging remain ledger operations.

## Cancellation subscriptions

`CancellationToken::subscribe() -> CancellationSubscription`
(`crates/verter_execution/src/cancellation.rs`) is the cancellation's own
pollable boundary. `poll(&mut self, &Waker)` answers `Ready` once the token
is cancelled and otherwise registers the waker under the token's waker
lock; `cancel` sets the flag and then takes and wakes every registration
outside that lock, so a cancellation racing a registration is never lost.
Dropping a subscription withdraws exactly its own registration and cancels
nothing.

An aggregate job token wakes its subscribers when it becomes cancelled:
when the last live owner's request cancels (each owner's request token
carries a registration that re-evaluates the aggregate) or when the last
live owner detaches. A requester cancelling while a sibling keeps the job
live wakes nothing. The waker lock is never taken while an aggregate's
owner list is held.

Existing owners attach atomically through `register_owners`, so cancellation
never observes only the cancelled prefix of a live batch. Under an enclosing
owner lock, `register_owner_deferred` and `register_owners_deferred` return
their notifications for delivery after that lock releases. Scheduler admission
uses the deferred batch form and its shared wake delivery rail.

Users: the semantic runtime registers on the token its stop selects
(`Program::wake_on_stop`, called whenever a poll returns `Pending`; for
`QueryProgram` the job token when it has owners, else the request's, else
the ownerless job's), so a pending `SemanticTask` is woken by the
cancellation that stops it. The scoped cache-node handoff registers on its
request's token while pending. The synchronous semantic flight wait, the
carrier publication and stable-parse lanes and the workspace flight wait
register on their cancellation and park untimed.

## The external-boundary driver

Compatibility entries that can answer on their first poll use
`block_on_when_pending`. They announce their wait family only after `Pending`.
The legacy boolean-cancellation and worker-pump boundaries delegate a pending
poll to `block_on` with their existing park interval; immediately ready answers
stay outside that timed drive. Owned dependency polls use their readiness and
stop subscriptions.

`verter_execution::blocking::block_on(family, slice, poll)` drives a
pollable computation to completion by blocking. It polls with a waker that
unparks the calling thread, and parks while the poll is pending. Each drive
retains its own notification, so a synchronous nested drive cannot consume
an outer computation's wake through the thread's shared park token. Every
driver over a cancellable wait passes no `slice`: the poll registers on its
cancellation, which wakes the thread. The `slice` parameter remains for a
caller that must bound its park for another reason.
`block_on_when_pending(family, poll)` is the same driver for a computation
that usually answers at once and waits only behind another caller's work
(the store-view read): it enters the `family` wait only when its first poll
is pending.
Semantic tasks register their cancellation wakes directly and do not require
timed re-polling.

Each synchronous entry listed below is the driver applied to the pollable
boundary beside it, so there is one implementation of each handoff, not a
blocking copy next to a polling one. The synchronous host entries drive
their roots ([Host roots](#host-roots)) as `WaitFamily::Root`. The semantic
runtime's synchronous root entry (`ProjectSemanticDispatch::drive_query`)
applies the same driver, untimed, to the root the production poll advances
([Polled root queries](#polled-root-queries)), and is woken by its stop's
cancellation. A semantic task itself has no blocking driver:
`SemanticTask::poll` is its only way forward.

The target is that only a synchronous public entry outside the computation
reaches the driver. The current tree does not meet it: the component-
metadata root still reaches the driver, and the other waits in
[Boundaries that still block](#boundaries-that-still-block), from deep
inside its resolution, and so does the macro traversal a compile root hands
its driver. No poll-facing value hands a computation a way to call it.

## Measuring what a root still reaches

Every synchronous wait on a dependency announces itself on entry through
`verter_execution::blocking::enter_wait(WaitFamily)`, before it checks
readiness, locks or parks; `block_on` takes the family as its first
argument and announces it. `WaitProbe::record()` lists the families the
installing thread enters; `WaitProbe::forbid()` makes entering any of them
panic, naming the family, before the wait has any effect. Probes cover the
installing thread only and nest (an inner probe under a forbidding one
forbids too). The probe is test evidence: it compiles only under `test` or
`verter_execution`'s `test-support` feature, and a production build keeps
each announcement as an empty inline call.

`verter_session`'s `root_continuations_tests` group runs the public roots

## Delivered boundaries

### Task-owned cancellation

`verter_execution::dependency::OwnedDependency<H>` binds a poll-only
`DependencyPoll` to its actual producer and waiter tasks. Both must belong
to the same registry. Pending registers one wait edge; a cycle, an already
waiting task or an authority mismatch returns `DependencyStop::Cycle`.
An immediately ready answer needs no edge. `DependencyStop::Cancelled`
contains no partial answer. Terminal answers and drop withdraw the readiness
and cancellation registrations, edge and task pins. No method exposes the
underlying handoff or its synchronous driver.

`CancellationToken::subscribe` observes explicit stop without timer-based
re-polling. Registration and stop publication share the token's wake lock.
Aggregate tokens forward the final owner's cancellation or withdrawal;
surviving owners keep the shared computation live. Liveness probes remain
callback-free. Owners that publish cancellation under an outer lock use
`cancel_deferred`, `register_owner_deferred` or
`CancellationOwner::detach_deferred` and deliver the
returned wakes after unlocking. The scheduler uses its existing DAG/lifecycle
delivery rail for these wakes as well as completion wakes.

The host constructs one registry and injects it into its scheduler and
semantic store. A scoped cache flight owns a producer task in that registry;
its producer installs that task while executing, including when moved to
another thread. A scoped subscriber captures the current task or owns a new
task for an external caller. It retains its wait edge while the transferred
producer runs under its own task, so a demand back into the waiting parent
is refused. Terminalization withdraws that edge. Cancellation
wakes the subscriber directly, and `Reentrant` also represents refused
cross-family cycles.

Terminal scoped handoffs also release their scheduler, identity and request
context; keeping a completed handoff alive does not retain its scheduler tree.

The semantic task retains cancellation subscriptions beside its suspended
execution. A request stop or the final shared-job owner's cancellation or
withdrawal wakes its latest poller, including after a move to another thread.
A job that has accepted request owners decides cancellation for shared work;
otherwise the bound request decides, with an ownerless job as fallback. The
job subscription remains armed while it is ownerless, so accepting owners
during a suspension cannot lose a later stop. An unselected ownerless job
may cause a harmless extra wake. Every terminal poll or task drop withdraws
the task's stop registrations. This does not make the nested synchronous
semantic callers in the inventory below pollable.

Semantic memo subscriptions use one polling implementation for both task
polls and synchronous joins. `Subscription::wait` applies the external
driver to `Subscription::poll`; flight cells hold only readiness wakers,
with no separate condition-variable wait implementation. The synchronous
join registers on its selected cancellation before each poll and parks
untimed.

Conditional frames retain completed operand preparation in
`ConditionalDecisionState`. The synchronous and frame entries use the same
preparation and selection stages. Each stage owns its speculative observation
scope: an unanswered read discards only that unfinished stage, while completed
preparation has already joined the frame's retained facts and taint. A later
relation dependency therefore resumes selection without preparing the operands
again. Incomplete preparation and selection still retry their own stage; this
does not make the remaining nested synchronous calls pollable.

### Scheduler completions

| Boundary | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| `CompletionHandle::subscribe() -> CompletionSubscription<T>` | `crates/verter_scheduler/src/job.rs` |
| `CompletionSubscription::poll(&mut self, &Waker) -> Poll<CompletionState<T>>` | same |

- **Result:** the handle's terminal `CompletionState`, which is `Ready`,
  `Failed`, `Superseded` or `Shutdown`. Every poll that observes it gets a
  clone, and re-polling after the answer keeps returning the same state.
- **Errors and cancellation:** a scheduler error, a supersession or a
  shutdown arrives as the terminal state itself. Dropping a pending
  subscription withdraws its registration and cancels nothing: sibling
  subscribers and blocking waiters on the same handle are unaffected.
- **Lifetime:** the subscription holds an `Arc` of the handle's slot, so the
  completion outlives the request that issued it. `CompletionSender::send`
  stays first-writer-wins. It wakes every registered subscription once,
  after releasing the slot's lock.
- **Scheduler lifecycle locks:** admission, stage completion, failure and
  retirement publish their terminal state while holding the DAG lock, and
  retain the taken wakers on that guard. The guard releases the DAG lock
  before delivering them. Reset additionally defers delivery until its
  serialization hold and driver join finish. State publication order and
  first-writer semantics therefore stay intact without invoking a completion
  callback inside those critical sections. Exclusively owned standalone DAGs
  deliver immediately. If this scheduler's own driver re-enters an already
  active reset, that reset retains the quiesce obligation and the driver
  returns without waiting on its joiner. The exemption uses the exact driver
  thread identity; another scheduler's driver still waits for the reset
  barrier.
- **Own-driver reset and restart:** when no external reset owns the barrier,
  a reset from a completion wake clears state and leaves shutdown set. The
  current driver stops after that callback returns; its identity and join
  handle remain owned by the scheduler. External restart joins it before
  reopening work. A reset followed by restart on that same driver reopens
  its existing loop, without spawning another driver. Native pump teardown
  rejects queued requests and dispatches no new stages while shutdown is
  set. A reset racing a healthy lifecycle hold waits in bounded parked
  slices; it returns early only once shutdown proves the holder may be
  joining this callback. Startup gates all work until identity and handle
  publication and lifecycle unlock. Joins hold no driver-handle mutex.
  `scheduler::reset_tests` exercises these paths using real completion wakes
  and retains cleanup guards that release dependencies and join test threads.
- **Backpressure:** none at completion; admission has its own handoff below.

`CompletionSubscription::owned(waiter, producer, cancellation)` returns an
`OwnedDependency` with the same terminal states. The supplied producer must
be the execution that actually produces the completion. It stops only that
subscriber, preserving the handle's first-writer-wins result and siblings.

`CompletionSubscription::owned_producer(waiter, cancellation)` follows the
scheduler's actual producer instead of accepting one from the caller. Gated
stages follow their first admitted prerequisite recursively, in dependency-key
order. Analysis dependencies not yet admitted follow their Source producer.
Each prerequisite release, new stage admission or scoped producer replacement
updates affected ancestor completions through the reverse dependency indexes
and wakes their subscriptions after the DAG lock releases. Re-polling moves
the root's single wait edge to that producer; completion and cancellation
release its readiness registration, stop registration and task pins.

`Scheduler::begin_owned_request` combines pollable inbox admission with this
completion ownership. Cancellation before admission drops the unsubmitted
request; cancellation after admission detaches only the waiter. Shared stages
and sibling requests continue.

### Host load and external integration

`VerterHost::begin_load` returns `HostLoadHandoff`. Its poll advances owned
scheduler admission/completion and transfers `HostLoadCommit` once Analysis is
ready. It performs no host integration, stage execution or blocking submission.
`HostLoadCommit::apply` performs integration externally and refuses another
host, cancellation, changed source incarnation/generation or a changed canonical
eviction revision or project authority. This fence also applies to warm commits
and prevents an old commit reversing a later eviction. Unrelated-file evictions
and additive load-generation changes remain compatible. The same stop, source,
project and revision fence is rechecked before any integration writes, under
the existing host admission fence shared with source replacement and eviction.
The fence permits same-thread nested contributor loading during ingress while
excluding concurrent writers. Ambient ingestion takes admission before its
snapshot cursor, matching upsert's lock order even when ingestion loads a file.
Workspace edges and aliases land before the dependency row marks integration
complete. A refused cold commit leaves no integrated marker; a superseded commit
cannot overwrite replacement dependencies. The caller assignments above this
handoff are DISPATCH for shared resolver loading, META for metadata traversal
and COMPILE for compilation.

The synchronous `ensure_loaded` entry drives this same handoff. Its external
driver rechecks cancellation after yielding, parks external native callers on
the driver, and lets scheduler workers use the existing cooperative pump and
bounded park cadence. Inline drivers retain typed `NoProgress` when no work
can advance. These driving operations are absent from the handoff capability.

### Carrier publication and stable parse

`CarrierPublicationStore::begin_publication` takes owned accepted source and
request context plus a waiter task, and returns `OwnedPublication`. Its poll
transfers `PublicationStep::Produce(PublicationProducer)` or returns a terminal
`Outcome`. The independent producer polls a retained continuation: it transfers
`PublicationProducerStep::Work(PublicationWork)` for the initial retained-unit
read/adoption, the nested stable producer, and envelope finishing, then returns
`Complete`. Only external `Work::run` performs backend reads, validation of
retained payloads, parsing, rehoming or retention. No poll drives another
computation synchronously. `publish_or_get` externally drives these same states.

`begin_stable_parse` returns `OwnedStableParse`. Its `Produce` step transfers
one `StableParseProducer`; `run` performs the double-checked retained read or
one parser attempt. `Parsed(StableParseValue)` owns the complete immutable
product and a retirement interest. A publication producer waits on this child
under its own P task, giving W → P → S in the host's single task registry.
Every store constructor takes its composition root's `TaskRegistry`. Host
construction supplies the same registry as scheduling and semantic evaluation;
standalone callers supply their execution authority explicitly. P/S always
belong to that registry, including synchronous production and reentrant backend
calls. Foreign pending waiters receive `Cycle`; an immediately ready answer
needs no edge. Registry binding cannot be replaced on an existing cloned store.
The synchronous wrapper inherits an ambient task only from that store's
registry; an unrelated host scope does not prevent independent publication.

Request cancellation stops only its subscriber, including the initiating
subscriber after producer transfer. P and S install independent task and stop
scopes during external work, restore them before notifications, and may move
between threads. Another subscriber can claim an unclaimed producer after its
initiator detaches. Terminal/drop cleanup releases its own edge, stop and wake
registrations and task pins. `ProducerDropped` is explicit abandonment;
after existing subscribers drain, the exact failed lane retires so unchanged
source can retry. It cannot remove a successor lane or invalidate another
subscriber's terminal.
`WinnerPanicked` remains distinct, and stable rejection carries `SyntaxReject`.
Source or grammar invalidation fences every snapshot-bound envelope, including
adopted and shared products; a verified stable unit remains reusable despite a
losing generation. Retention denial still returns the complete envelope.

Successful publication delivery stays strong while attached consumers remain,
then returns to the existing weak terminal retention. Attaching to a completed
lane pins its live envelope atomically with subscription registration, before
the first poll; if the envelope already expired, attachment restarts production.
Stable lanes stay listed
through each registered consumer's envelope finishing. The retirement interest
moves into the actual Finish Work and survives dropping the P continuation;
retirement rechecks consumer/producer state under table → lane locks and removes
only the identical lane instance. Backend callbacks and wakes run after owner
locks release. Adoption provenance, parser counters and snapshot rehoming use
the existing helpers.

Callers above these boundaries belong to DISPATCH for the scheduler Source
stage and shared base/overlay indexing, META for metadata traversal, and COMPILE
for compile hydration. Their conversion is separate from these dependency APIs.

### Scheduler request admission

`Scheduler::begin_submit_request` returns an owned
`SchedulerSubmission<CompletionSubscription<RequestResult>>`.
`begin_submit_batch_atomic` returns the same handoff over a vector of
subscriptions in input order. Neither the handoff nor its output exposes a
blocking completion driver. Its only advancing operation is `poll(&mut self,
&Waker)`.

- **Backpressure:** a full inbox retains the whole submission (one item for
  an atomic batch) and returns `Pending`, without draining the inbox or
  executing a stage. The offer and registration share the capacity lock;
  consumers take the registrations after dequeue and wake after unlock.
- **Result and errors:** `Ready` transfers the completion subscription(s).
  Inbox admission is distinct from stage completion; poll the subscriptions
  separately. If shutdown rejects admission, those subscriptions are already
  terminal with `Shutdown`. An empty batch returns an empty vector without
  queue or submission accounting. Re-polling a completed admission panics.
- **Lifetime and cancellation:** construction captures each request's file
  lifetime using the same preparation as synchronous submission. The handoff
  retains the scheduler and all unsubmitted inputs. Dropping it withdraws
  only its own capacity registration and discards unsubmitted work. Once
  admitted, dropping a completion subscription leaves sibling consumers and
  the producer intact; request-context cancellation keeps its existing
  scheduler semantics. Reset invalidates the captured file lifetime, so a
  retained request cannot republish a retired incarnation after reset.
- **External drivers:** `submit_request`, `submit_batch_atomic` and the
  scheduler's internal synchronous sends drive the same admission state.
  Only these compatibility drivers may drain an older inbox item to avoid
  parking the sole consumer behind its own full queue. Stage execution stays
  with the existing scheduler driver or an explicitly driven `drive_one`.
  This handoff introduces no executor or pool.

### Scoped cache-node admission and producer transfer

`Scheduler::begin_scoped_cache_node<T>` returns `ScopedCacheNodeHandoff<T>`.
Its `poll` yields while the bounded inbox is full, until DAG dispatch, or
while another caller produces the shared value. It never executes a stage,
waits, enters a pool or runs a producer. The synchronous
`execute_scoped_cache_node` drives this same handoff and runs its transferred
producer on the existing CPU pool.

- **Producer ownership:** the first eligible poll after dispatch receives
  `ScopedCacheNodeStep::Produce`. That intermediate result transfers one
  `ScopedCacheNodeProducer<T>` to the external driver. Its `run` installs the
  request context, aggregate cancellation and active-path identity, executes
  the supplied closure, contains panic and publishes once. Dropping it
  without running settles the flight as cancelled. A later poll of the
  handoff receives the terminal `Value(Arc<T>)`; every surviving follower
  receives that same value. Producers can borrow the external caller's data.
- **Task ownership and cycles:** the host injects its single `TaskRegistry`
  into both the scheduler and semantic store. The explicit scheduler
  constructors are `with_executor_and_task_registry` (threaded native) and
  `new_sync_with_executor_and_task_registry` (inline native/WASM); standalone
  constructors create a registry for their scheduler. A handoff captures the
  installed task at construction, or registers a new external-root task. Its
  producer retains and installs that task wherever `run` executes, restoring
  the destination thread's prior task afterwards. A follower holds one RAII
  edge to the elected producer in that same registry. Same-task recursion or
  a cross-task cycle returns the existing `Reentrant` refusal to that follower;
  surviving owners keep the producer. Observing completion/refusal or dropping
  the handoff releases its edge. Election wakes earlier subscribers to register their
  edges now that the producer is known. The handoff that transferred the
  producer may await its external execution without a self-edge. Wakes still
  schedule later polls and run beyond the flight/lifecycle holds.
- **Backpressure and lifetime:** admission retains the unsubmitted item and
  separate capacity and flight registrations. A sibling's settlement
  can bypass that item's capacity wait; a retired flight resolves
  even if the successor inbox is full. The handoff and producer retain the
  scheduler independently and can move between threads with exclusive
  polling. Dropping a handoff withdraws its registrations and detaches only
  its owner; a surviving sibling keeps shared work live. Attaching owners
  collected before admission holds a temporary aggregate registration until
  every request is attached, so an already-cancelled request cannot retire
  the flight ahead of an existing live sibling.
- **Errors:** cancellation, shutdown/reset, panic, mismatched result type and
  same-path re-entry retain the existing typed outcomes. Cancellation is
  checked and registered at each poll. Cancelling the request wakes its
  subscriber, whose next poll answers `Cancelled` without disturbing the
  producer or sibling followers. Shared-registry wait edges refuse cycles
  before suspension. Polling after a terminal result is a contract violation.
- **Wake ordering:** dispatch and settlement take registrations under the
  flight lock. Delivery uses the scheduler's deferral rail, after flight,
  scoped-incarnation and DAG locks release, including reset's outer hold.
  Wakes request a later poll and do not execute the producer.
- **Evidence:** `scheduler::scoped_handoff_tests` exercises yielding before
  queue/result release, cross-thread producer transfer, follower isolation,
  panic/drop, re-entry, reset with a full successor inbox, and unlocked wake
  delivery. The session compile contract
  `scoped_handoff_no_blocking_scheduler` denies recovering the blocking
  scheduler from the handoff.
  `scoped_cache_handoffs_share_the_semantic_wait_graph` holds real semantic
  and scoped producers, closes a cycle in both dependency orders, and runs
  the surviving producer on another thread under its original task.
- **Production use.** The compile root awaits the Vue macro bundle's node
  through this handoff and hands its producer and dispatch steps to its
  driver as `RootWork` ([Host roots](#host-roots)); the synchronous macro
  bundle entry and the Vue API projector still use
  `execute_scoped_cache_node`. Both drivers share one request, one producer
  body and one delivery (`vue_macro_codegen_request`,
  `build_vue_macro_codegen`, `delivery_of`, `finish_vue_macro_codegen`),
  and both place producers through `Scheduler::run_transferred_producer`.
  The scheduler contract is unchanged: followers of a dropped producer
  settle `Cancelled` and the scheduler starts nothing. The macro bundle's
  callers decide whether to take a delivered value or begin the node again.

### Store-view reads

`StoreViewManager::base_view_pollable` (`crates/verter_session/src/resolver_store.rs`)
is a borrowed compatibility adapter over the same claim core as
`VerterHost::begin_store_view`. A warm hit answers at once; a reader behind

runs the producer before continuing. Callers that must also transfer that

supersession fingerprint; the existing post-capture comparison still
rejects a snapshot straddling an edit, and a return-only read still blocks
warm reuse and stable promotion. The synchronous currentness accessor
drives this same computation outside a poll.

- **Lifetime and cancellation.** Dropping a pending read withdraws its
  registration, task edge and test-only parked-reader count. The borrowed

- **Evidence:** `root_continuations_component_meta_root_suspends_behind_a_held_store_view_build`

  `dropping_a_component_meta_request_withdraws_its_pending_snapshot` drops

### Resolver request flights

`SingleflightGroup::begin_owned_retaining` returns `OwnedResolverFlight`.
Its only advancing operation, `poll`, returns a complete
`SingleflightRunResult` or transfers the sole `OwnedResolverProducer`.
Polling does not compute or run the retention policy. `run_retaining` and
`run` externally drive the same core with borrowed callbacks.
Synchronous and transferred producers own distinct tasks. A pollable
computation retains its polling root's task as the lane owner across suspension.

- **Ownership:** host composition binds every resolver flight family to the
  host's existing registry. Production compatibility entries install that
  authority. Standalone synchronous default groups reuse ambient task authority
  when available, otherwise lazily establish their group's registry so
  cross-thread wait cycles retain the inline computation escape.
  A transferred producer installs its actual distinct task and
  installs a live aggregate cancellation owner while running. A cancelled waiter
  whose thread executes the producer cannot stop shared work through either
  its job or request token. Other request accounting stays installed.
  Compatibility drivers retain ambient cancellation. The waiting parent keeps its edge until
  terminalization or drop.
- **Cancellation and errors:** cancellation stops one subscriber without
  stopping its producer or siblings. `Cycle`, `ProducerUnowned`,
  `ProducerDropped`, `ProducerPanicked`, `Reset` and the original producer
  error are typed terminal refusals. The owned producer contains panic;
  compatibility producers preserve unwinding and their followers re-elect. Same-path
  owned recursion refuses a cycle; synchronous recursion retains its nested
  computation escape outside poll.
- **Publication:** `participate_owned` pins before a warm-cache probe and
  can survive suspension without borrowing the group. Participation pins retain a stable rendezvous for its
  concurrent burst. Non-retained results publish to existing followers and
  leave the table in one locked operation. Pointer-checked retirement cannot
  remove a successor flight. Reset wakes a held producer's subscribers and
  prevents its later execution or publication.
- **Lifetime and backpressure:** each subscriber owns one readiness and stop
  registration and exactly its own task edge. Terminalization withdraws them
  before dropping joined values, retained callbacks, keys, group handles and
  task pins. Producer transfer has no queue or pool admission. Dropping an unrun producer wakes
  followers with a typed failure; dropping a follower leaves siblings live.

The metadata request and fallthrough flight callers belong to metadata
traversal conversion. Module exports, overlay materialization, prepared
and indexed declarations, imported sources, route names and barrel surfaces
belong to dispatch conversion; compilation reaches them through dispatch.
Their existing synchronous calls still need resumable traversal state.

### Base store-view capture

`VerterHost::begin_store_view(waiter, cancellation)` returns the opaque,
owned `StoreViewHandoff`. `poll` returns `StoreViewStep::Produce` with an
independent `Send` producer or `Value` with the capture's intrinsic currentness.
It never builds a snapshot or sweeps retired artifacts. A cold producer's
external `run` builds and publishes through the existing manager cache and
its exact token/reset fence. A completed producer reply revalidates the full
live token and cached snapshot identity under the manager lock when its owner
resumes; publication alone cannot mint currentness after suspension. A warm
hit returns immediately unless released roots left reclamation pending; then the same claim transfers maintenance
with the exact cached read, preserving a return-only read's reason.

The manager uses the host's shared registry and one physical claim. Each
subscription retains its own wait edge and stop registration. Typed errors
are `Cancelled`, `Cycle`, `ProducerUnowned`, `ProducerDropped`,
`ProducerPanicked` and `Reset`; none carries a partial view. Dropping or
cancelling a subscriber leaves its transferred producer and siblings live.
An abandoned producer releases only its exact claim, and owned execution
contains panic after settling subscribers. Readiness and reset take wakers
under the manager lock and invoke them after unlocking.

Reset revokes unstarted work immediately. Running work keeps the physical
claim until it exits, while pre-reset subscribers become `Reset` at once.
New subscribers wait for that physical release, then recapture under the new
generation; they do not inherit the old reset result. Successful followers
recapture and validate the cache under the original bounded main/final/fallback
rounds. The synchronous `StoreViewManager::base_view` drives this same core,
retaining its execution-scoped reentry refusal and external reclamation.
Completed handoffs release their host, task and cancellation interests; the
opaque result retains only its captured view and exposes no blocking driver.
When a subscriber releases the final host, it pins the lowering service through
host teardown, then closes its queues without joining. Already admitted jobs
drain on their existing workers, so cancellation never waits for those jobs.
The owned poll also pins that service through its transient cached views and
result mapping, covering warm completion and failed production cleanup.
Ordinary external service owners retain their joining release semantics.

Caller conversion above this boundary remains with DISPATCH for shared
`capture_batch_fixed_view`/resolver contexts, META for component metadata
traversal, and COMPILE for compile traversal. Overlay application and batch
membership ingestion remain caller operations, outside this base capture.

### Declaration-lowering owner-shard handoffs

Retained parses live on dedicated worker shards and never leave them. A job
carries owned inputs to the shard and brings back owned results.

| Pollable boundary | Synchronous entry over it | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DeclLoweringService::begin_acquire_lease -> LeaseHandoff<()>` | `acquire_lease` | `(LeaseOutcome, None)` |
| `DeclLoweringService::begin_acquire_lease_and_run -> LeaseHandoff<R>` | `acquire_lease_and_run` | `(LeaseOutcome, Option<R>)` |
| `DeclLoweringService::begin_run_leased -> LeasedRunHandoff<R>` | `run_leased` | `Option<R>`, where `None` is a lease miss |
| `DeclLoweringService::begin_run_leased_batch -> LeasedBatchHandoff<R>` | `run_leased_batch` | `Vec<Option<R>>` in input order |

All of these are in `crates/verter_semantic_source/src/decl_lowering.rs`.

Each handoff additionally offers `owned(waiter, cancellation)` before its
first poll. This returns `OwnedLoweringHandoff`, whose poll yields
`LoweringStep::Work` or `Value`, with typed `LoweringFailure` (`WorkerStopped`,
`ProducerDropped`, `Panicked` or `Reentrant`) inside its value. The existing worker job
installs its producer task from the waiter's registry; no parser arena or
borrowed AST crosses a shard boundary. A batch's pure shard operations
jointly produce one ordered answer under its batch task identity. Cancellation
drops unsubmitted jobs or abandons admitted replies; an arriving lease is
released on its owner shard. Existing synchronous entries retain their panic
compatibility and drive the same queue/reply state.

On native workers the owned step normally carries `Value`. WASM transfers a
`Send` `LoweringWork` token and polls a retained reply; no parser work or pure
callback runs inside poll. External `run` contains operation panic, and dropping
the token settles `ProducerDropped`. The shared dependency remains pending
across this transfer, retaining its wait edge, task and stop interest until the
value or stop is observed. Cancelling the subscriber after transfer leaves that
work independent. Raw handoffs expose `poll_step` for the same transfer, and
synchronous entries run it outside the reply poll. An empty batch is ready
without work or an arena borrow. The generic inline reply/work protocol has
native behavioral controls as well as target compilation; its fields carry
owned operations and answers, never an AST or parser arena.

The executing worker opens its existing shard identity on that actual task.
An owned demand targeting a shard its waiter already owns returns `Reentrant`
before queue admission. Batches preflight every target before admitting any
share. WASM checks its single arena identity before borrowing the thread-local
shard. This is a defensive refusal; callbacks remain pure and may not reenter
the service or host.

- **Backpressure yields.** A poll first offers the job to its worker's
  bounded queue with a non-blocking send. If the queue is full, the poll
  keeps the job, registers for the space the worker frees when it dequeues
  a job, and returns `Pending`. The offer and the registration happen under
  the queue's waiter lock, and the worker takes that lock only after a
  dequeue, so space freed in between still wakes the submitter. No send
  ever blocks behind a poll.
- **Result:** once the queue admits the job, a poll takes the worker's answer
  or registers for it in the reply's lock. The worker's answer takes that
  same lock. A job that panicked on the worker re-panics on the polling
  thread. For a lease acquisition, it re-panics only after the lease was
  handed back, so the pin is released as the panic unwinds. If a worker
  stops without answering, the reply is closed and the poll panics, as the
  blocking rendezvous it replaced did.
- **Cancellation and lifetime:** dropping a handoff withdraws whichever
  registration it holds and abandons its reply. If the answer has already
  arrived, it is dropped. If it arrives later, the worker drops it on its
  own thread. Either way, a lease acquired for an abandoned handoff is
  released on the shard that pinned it, leaving no abandoned lease or parse
  retained after shard cleanup. While a handoff is outstanding it holds the
  service, and so its workers, alive. After releasing unclaimed answers and
  unsubmitted closures, a final handoff atomically extracts the service and
  closes its queues without joining workers. Already-admitted jobs finish
  independently and their workers release shard state on exit. Even cancelling
  the final unsubmitted subscriber cannot enter worker drainage inside poll.
  A final external service owner retains joining shutdown; a worker dropping
  the final service handle joins only other workers. Polling after the answer
  is a contract violation.
- **Batches:** each poll advances every share still outstanding, so every
  shard's share is offered on the first poll and the shards lower in
  parallel. The batch answers once every share has answered, and the first
  job panic re-panics then.
- **wasm32:** the shard remains thread-local. Construction retains owned
  inputs; an owned poll or raw `poll_step` transfers `LoweringWork` before
  execution. External `run` installs its actual task and opens the arena
  identity around the pure operation. Later polls observe only its reply.
  Stop before transfer executes nothing; stop after transfer abandons only
  the subscriber. Panic containment requires an unwinding target; the default
  wasm panic-abort target is checked for compilation rather than recovery.

### Polled root queries

`ThreadDispatch::poll_query(key)` begins a root query as a
`SemanticQueryPoll`, the production entry a consumer that must not block
drives a query through. It borrows its dispatcher exclusively and stays on
the dispatcher's thread. `SemanticQueryPoll::poll` returns `Ready` with the
full-value read the synchronous `execute_via_cold_build_helper` returns for
the same key, in the query's own value domain (a flow return, a call
resolution, a relation or a classification is delivered as itself, never
narrowed to a type node), or `Pending` once the query waits on another
task's producer that will wake the poller. `SemanticQueryPoll::poll_node`
is the explicit type-node adapter; it narrows exactly as `execute_read`
does. The entry also implements `Future`, with the full-value read as its
output.

The entry and the synchronous entry run one implementation. An
instantiation, a `typeof`, a machinery-root conditional, call or relation, or a
callback-typing machinery-root flow return enters as a driven root: the root's connected demand, its query level and the
dispatcher operation it runs as are opened once and held across polls,
because the request state they live on moves into the lent task until the
task gives it back. The thread's execution scopes (audit attribution,
construction scope, validity) are entered for each poll. `drive_query`
blocks on the same root with the external-boundary driver.
The root claims its family memo and publishes the same canonical candidate as
the synchronous entry. Only dependency frames may complete a cheap intermediate
for the request alone, outside that memo.

Every other query enters as a cold root through the synchronous entry's own
cold-query admission (`begin_cold_query`: canonical key, sealed isolated-root
refusal, carrier-subject normalization, entry charge) and family-memo claim
(`claim_cold_query`), under the same dispatcher operation, entered once and
held across polls. The claim's acquisition (`SemanticGraphStore::acquire_query`)
takes an `AcquireWait`: the synchronous entry passes `Block` and waits another
task's producer out, the polled entry passes `Yield`, which leaves the claim
and its subscription in a `PendingAcquire` slot with the poll's waker
registered on the producer's flight and returns `Pending`. The claim keeps the
execution task it waits as across polls and installs it for each; the next poll
continues the same claim — its lookup is not repeated — and either reads the
producer's result or claims again and produces. A produced read is finished by
the same `finish_cold_query` tail, then re-rooted where it is delivered; a
flow return off the runtime drains or aborts its completed members as its
synchronous executor does. Dropping a waiting cold root withdraws its wait and
closes its entry, then its operation.

While the entry is pending, `SemanticQueryPoll::task` lends its
`SemanticTask`. The task is `Send` and has one exclusive poller, so it may
be advanced to readiness on another thread with `SemanticTask::poll`. It
keeps its root's delivery, and the entry delivers the delivery on its own
thread at its next poll. Neither value exposes the dispatcher, the resolver
capabilities or a blocking driver. Dropping a pending entry releases the
task's frames, flights and waits, gives the request state back within the
execution's scopes and then closes the root's operation.

Contracts: `semantic_query_poll_consumer` (positive: poll the entry, advance
its task on another thread), `semantic_task_no_blocking_driver` and
`semantic_query_poll_no_dispatcher` (negative). Behaviour:
`a_polled_root_query_suspends_resumes_elsewhere_and_reads_what_the_synchronous_entry_reads`
and `a_polled_root_query_released_while_pending_leaves_nothing_open` in the
session's semantic-task tests, and its `semantic_poll_contract` group: a
cold root whose key another task produces yields while held and then reads
what the synchronous full-value entry reads, receipt included; a released
waiting cold root leaves nothing open; roots answering outside the type-node
domain poll their own value; and a single-threaded control polls a chain to
the synchronous read with no other thread advancing it. A distributed
conditional holds a selected branch's dependency after a sibling completes,
resumes its task on a different thread, and compares exact evidence and logical
work with the synchronous boundary. Its production counters also require each
member fold to execute once. A direct call root holds an argument alias during
inference and delivers the full call result after resuming. The same group runs
with optional capture compiled in (`semantic-observe`). The WASM controls
`semantic_poll_contract_*` in `verter_wasm` drive the production entry on the
single-threaded `wasm32-unknown-unknown` host: a chain, a conditional dependency
and a cold root each yield instead of parking the only thread and deliver what
the synchronous entry serves.

The entry is only as non-blocking as the frames below it. A root's own claim
yields, but a query a frame step or a cold build evaluates in place still
reaches the waits under
[Boundaries that still block](#boundaries-that-still-block).

### Family continuations

A family whose build reads other queries or files can be written as an owned
sequential continuation (`project_semantic_dispatch/family_continuation.rs`).
Its first stage either answers or names the one dependency it reads next —
a query (`FamilyStep::Need`), a file's indexed input (`FamilyStep::Load`)
or a resolver port (`FamilyStep::Port`, below) — and retains everything it

only a step the inventories settle unambiguously: the scope's own import
binding, a named re-export, an export of a local import binding, or a
file's sole `export *`; a hop never follows a private import. Anything
else — two or more wildcards, an unresolved specifier, an unservable hop,
a namespace import, an `export =`, an ambient module — asks the in-place
bare-name resolver as a port, which applies the full route policy, so the
declaration the lookup settles on is the one it always settled on), macro payloads
(`ResolveMacroPayload`: owner file, then the props intersection or the
emits/slots/model projection), ordered intersection reductions
(`ReduceIntersection`: each nested subgroup), overload sets
(`ResolveOverloadSet`: the callee's
`SignaturesOfType` list, then the `ReadSignatureResult` each composite
candidate's node form applies, one at a time; the list is read once — a
list a kernel-epoch replacement retired is read through the epoch it was
published in, and a composite whose result read was named in a retired
epoch is named again after one pass copies its descriptor into the current
epoch; with explicit type arguments, each
candidate of the settled list in turn through explicit instantiation,
below, the ones that cannot take the arguments dropping) and apparent types (`ApparentType`:
the anchor walk's signature lists of an object surface, the base's call and
construct lists choosing a `strictBindCallApply` interface, the global
interface lookup with its `Function` fallback — itself a continuation,

and each indexed, unobserved, as its own transition (what the population read would otherwise make
IndexedReady in place), the program's contributor population as a port
and a `declare global` contributor's module as a load — then the interface's
`Instantiate`) and class instance surfaces (`ResolveClassSurface` on its

this way.

  a converted family.
- **Retained loads.** A load a family waited on joins the frame's consumed
  loads whichever way it ended (`request_ports::retain_consumed_load`), so
  every later read of the file by the same build — a later stage's load,
  a port's prepared-declaration or route read, a scope read — answers from
  the retained delivery or failure and never begins the load again
  (`consumed_load_known`). Readers nest: an inner frame's reads see an
  enclosing frame's retained outcomes beneath its own.

- **One implementation, two drivers.** The synchronous producer (the cold
  build every synchronous entry runs) drives the continuation in place,
  reading each named dependency where the build stands. A query frame drives
  the same stages by needing each named query, so another task's producer of
  it suspends the frame with the build owned by the frame
  (`FrameBuild::Family`), and the delivery resumes it. A family read from an
  entry outside every open checker obligation drives on the runtime from its
  synchronous entry as well, so both entries price their reads alike.
- **Retained entry admission.** A projection whose entry canonicalizes its
  sugar (`ProjectMember`, `IndexedAccess`) or normalizes its subject (a
  `BareRef`, `ImportType` or `typeof` carrier; a scope-less subject read
  through its apparent wrapper or apparent signatures) is admitted before
  it is claimed by an owned computation (`query_entry::QueryEntryBuild`):
  sugar, then the carrier's resolution (`carrier::CarrierRead`), then the
  wrapper's global lookup and instantiation, then the prelude. Each stage
  names the query, port or file it reads next and retains the admission's
  tracer, the carrier's held observation (`ObservedRead<()>`, reopened for
  the next stage) and what it settled; the carrier stays on the
  dispatcher's normalization record (the pre-memo cycle guard) until it
  settles or its holder abandons it. One computation, three drivers: the
  synchronous entry admits in place; a polled root
  (`RootPollState::Admitting`) polls each admission read as a nested root,
  and each file its lookup waits on as a frame polls it,
  and enters the admitted key, with the connected demand and isolated-root
  identity it kept, only once the admission settles; a frame whose family
  needs such a projection admits it in the frame (`FrameBuild::Admitting`),
  each admission read a need of that frame, and then needs the admitted key
  (`FrameBuild::FamilyAdmitted`). The prelude travels with the admitted key
  to the frame that produces it, whose publication merges its facts and
  verdict, and the reader attributes the delivered read with it, as the
  synchronous entry does. Admission is no query of its own and records no
  receipt. Work is counted per stage
  (`SemanticGraphStats::admission_stages_walked`).
- **File loads.** `IndexedInputs::begin_indexed_load` answers whether a file
  is servable without loading it, or returns an owned `IndexedLoad` over the
  host's `begin_load` handoff, which integrates the file when Analysis is
  ready exactly as `ensure_loaded` does. On a production poll's drive a frame
  waits on that load as an outside input (`EvalStep::Await`, which steps the
  same frame again once the load resolves) with its build retained
  (`FrameBuild::FamilySource`); the synchronous entry's drive loads the file
  in place as its external driver. The load's end is a typed terminal
  `IndexedLoadOutcome` the frame consumes once: an integrated file is
  served as loaded, and a load that ended without integrating it (absent,
  failed, cancelled, superseded, a cycle, shutdown) resumes the build with no
  input for the file — what the synchronous serve answers after the same
  failed load. The frame never begins that load again and never serves the
  file in place to rediscover the failure. An explicit session overlay or a
  tombstoned file needs no load. A scheduler without a driver thread
  (every wasm32 host) has no executor but the caller, so nothing would
  advance a load begun for a frame to wait on: there the serve loads the
  file in place, executing its stages on the polling thread, exactly as
  the synchronous load does.
- **Stage capability.** Every stage is a method of `ContinuationStage`, which
  holds the dispatcher in a field private to `family_continuation` and
  exposes only graph construction, the construction meter, canonical
  composite normalization, the project generation a fence pins and the
  self-roots its nodes observed. A stage therefore cannot read another
  query, read or load a file, join a producer or drive an execution; only
  the two drivers hold the dispatcher. Contracts:
  `continuation_stage_constructs_through_its_capability` (positive) and
  `continuation_stage_reaches_no_synchronous_adapter` (negative), in the
  session compile contracts.
- **No replay.** The per-graph counters `continuation_builds_begun` and
  `continuation_stages_resumed` count stages wherever they run, so a stage
  run again under either driver changes them.
- **Kept results stay valid where they are consumed.** The execution keeps
  a completed demand's value for later needs only while
  `Program::reusable` holds, and asks it again each time a need would take
  the kept value. The query program answers it with the memo's own warm
  gates: complete, cacheable, and naming no retired signature-kernel epoch
  (`SemanticGraphStore::serves_stored_value`). A list retired after it
  completed is therefore forgotten and that one demand started again; the
  family that needed it keeps its stage, and a list retired before it
  completed is never kept.
- **One epoch per computation; retired handles are rebound, never
  re-read.** Every kernel computation holds one `EpochBinding` for its
  whole life — a discovery walk the epoch current as it begins, a result
  read the epoch its key names, a list the epoch its delivered value was
  published in — and interns into it even after a replacement retires it.
  A delivered list is pinned through its own lease, so a list retired
  before or after it completed is read once. A composite candidate's
  `ReadSignatureResult` is named in the list's epoch; when a replacement
  retires that epoch while the read waits, the build reports it
  (`FamilyBuild::awaits_retired_read`). A result forced before the
  retirement is delivered and consumed where it was published (its own
  lease). Any other answer is not consumed — the frame drops the delivery
  unread, the in-place driver does not make the read — so the stale read's
  incompleteness never taints the family; the list then copies its
  unsettled composite descriptors into the current epoch in one pass
  (`EpochRebind`, a metadata copy that reads no query and waits on
  nothing) and names the read again. Each retirement costs one copy of
  what is still unsettled; the list, the composites it settled and their
  results are never read again. A walk whose epoch retired before it
  published copies its found set into the current epoch; the identity
  comparison of two sets read in different epochs copies the source's
  candidate into the target's. A discovery walk keeps its binding across
  its suspensions: a walk suspended while the kernel replaces its epoch
  finishes in the epoch it began in, and a stage never binds again.
- **Owned, exclusively advanced state.** `FamilyBuild` and `FamilyStep` are
  asserted `Send` and neither `Clone` nor `Copy`: a build is moved into each
  stage and out again, never shared between drivers. A polled root lends its
  task only through an exclusive borrow of its entry
  (`semantic_query_poll_consumer`, positive;
  `semantic_task_exclusive_poll`, negative: the entry cannot be polled while
  its task is out, and a shared task cannot be polled).

### Helper continuations

The relation authority and the call executor also run inline, as helpers
a computation calls under the checker state it holds: an open
obligation, an inference session, a binding barrier, a contextual call's
collector. A consumer that must not block runs such a helper as an owned
computation it advances
(`project_semantic_dispatch/helper_continuation.rs`):

- It begins the helper, which reads nothing: `RelationRead::pair`
  (`execute_relate_pair_kind`: a pair behind a binding barrier),
  `RelationRead::assignable` (`execute_relate_pair`, with the literal-union
  membership fast path), `RelationRead::member` (`execute_relate_with_occurrence`:
  a sub-relation depositing into the session open around it),
  `RelationRead::root` (the `Relate` family's build as its machinery root)
  and `CallRead::begin` (the call executor's contextual entry, continuing a
  `ContextualCallState`).
- It advances the helper with the delivery of the request it last stood at
  (`advance_relation_read`, `advance_relation_root`, `advance_call_read`,
  each taking a `HelperDrive`). The helper answers `HelperStep::Done` with
  its output, or `HelperStep::Request` with the one dependency it reads
  next, holding everything it computed before the read.
- A frame of the continuation runtime turns a request into its own need
  (`helper_request_poll` for a relation root, `call_request_poll` for a
  call, each keeping the request's entered claim for the runtime; a call's
  also keeps where the walk asked, and admits first a read whose entry
  admits it) and hands the delivery back (`helper_delivery`,
  `call_helper_delivery`, the latter with the admission the read was
  admitted through). The
  synchronous driver (`HelperDrive::InPlace`) makes each read in place, so
  the helper never stops. A consumer that stops while its helper waits
  releases it (`release_relation_read`, `release_call_read`), closing what
  the helper holds open.

`HelperRequest`, `HelperAwait` and `HelperDelivery` are opaque, owned and
`Send`; the helpers are `Send` and neither `Clone` nor `Copy`. A delivery
is the complete `QueryDelivery`, never a node narrowed out of it, boxed
with its key and any admission (`DeliveredRead`) so the delivery a helper
is advanced with is a pointer. The
request kinds grow as a helper's internal stages are converted, behind the
same opaque types and frame glue, so its consumers keep driving it the same
way. Besides query reads, a call has one request its consumer answers
itself: the callback exchange. A contextual call whose candidate names a
context-sensitive argument for the consumer to type requests it
(`HelperRequest::callback_at`: the argument's position and its contextual
type); the call keeps the candidate's collector in its continuation while
the consumer types it (`CallRead::contextual_mut` lends the continuation
for an object or array literal typed member by member), and the typed
arguments (`HelperDelivery::callback`) resume that collector. The
synchronous adapter (`drive_call_in_place`) answers the exchange as the
executor's step naming the argument, as before. The synchronous entries
already drive these computations in place:
`execute_relate_pair`, `execute_relate_pair_kind`, the call walk's
binding applicability relation and the flow evaluator's contextual call.
The memoized callable reads stay ordinary query keys a consumer needs:
`SignaturesOfType`, `ReadSignatureResult`, `ResolveOverloadSet`,
`AwaitedNormalize` and `AsyncReturnPayload`.

What converts today is a machinery relation root's structural dispatch.
Driven by a frame, the root's first stage validates its key and opens its
root obligation frame, owned by the computation as a `RelateRootBuild`,
and reduces the pair in place up to its structural dispatch (the axis
refusal, prefilters, projections, conditional readings, excess prepass,
spread programs and shallow checks, `reduce_relation_before_dispatch`).
When those leave the pair undecided, the dispatch's first reads — the
unwraps of both operands' identity carriers — are demanded
(`OperandDemand`): each operand's first instantiation, the read its carrier
walk makes first when it reaches it through graph-only steps
(`relation_operand_first_instantiation`, computed without reading), is
entered once as the walk would enter it; a warm result answers at once and
one that must be computed is a request. With every demanded instantiation
answered, the dispatch runs over the deliveries. Nothing is demanded that
the reduction would not read next, so a pair a shallow check decides
instantiates no operand, and nothing an operand's walk reads after its
first instantiation — a deeper hop, a deferred evaluation, a key set — is
read twice; those reads are still made in place.

The dispatch itself is an owned computation (`RelationDispatch`): its
prefix — carrier unwraps, recursion entry, alias normalization and the
other steps before the structural worklist
(`decide_relation_before_worklist`) — runs in place, and its worklist
(`RelateWorklist`: the items still to relate and the results so far) is
advanced by a loop that can stop. On a frame, two kinds of item stop it:
an object comparison (`ObjectWalk`: the target property it stands at, the
combined result of the properties related so far, the accessor keys
already related) at a property pair, and a canonical sub-relation
(`RelateWork::Eval` of a pair the canonical frame relates). An object
comparison the prefix's Object-vs-Record arm reaches (a declaration or
object source against a literal-key target) is handed to the loop as its
one item, held before its first property pair, so it stops the same way
(`BeforeWorklist::Objects`). Either item
reaches a sub-relation, which is staged exactly as the authority runs it
(`StagedMember`): the member steps before its key (a projection, an
inference deposit, an uncached union source), the SCC substitution rail,
the answers before compute (a literal pair, an overflowed chain, a
reentered frame and its back-edge, a settled or warm answer), then its
inline frame opened and its reduction run up to its structural dispatch.
When that leaves the pair undecided its operands' first instantiations
are demanded as a root's are; one that must be computed stops the
sub-relation, its frame open, and with it the object comparison, the
worklist and the root, each keeping where it stands. The delivery enters
the sub-relation's demand; with its operands answered its own dispatch
runs the same way — stopping again wherever its worklist does — and its
frame closes through the SCC discharge, its step folded onto the caller's
bindings, and the comparison continues at its next property: no property
relates again, no item is charged again, no frame opens again. Driven in
place (`relate_objects`, `decide_relation`), the same computations never
stop. A release closes the frames the stopped sub-relations opened,
innermost first, then the root's. A pair or member relation computes in
place on either driver. A `Relate` root polled at the production entry, or
read through the query entry outside every open obligation and any drive —
not a binding root, not a re-discharge, its symmetric operands already in
canonical order — is such a frame (`FrameBuild::Relation`): its root
obligation frame stays open across `Pending`, a release closes it
undecided, and its completion drains or aborts the members its reduction
completed against its own publication. The relation authority's own root
(`execute_relate_root`, behind every pair and member question) drives the
same root computation in place: relations are the engine's hottest root,
and a drive per root costs more than the reads it could suspend at. Its
reduction, inference, SCC discharge and a call's resolution still run in
place ([Boundaries that still block](#boundaries-that-still-block)).

A call's executor walk converts at its own seams
(`call_resolve.rs`, `CallWalk`). The walk is an owned computation
(`advance_call_walk`): it substitutes its callee, reads its candidate
lists and acquires its arguments once (`PlanStage`), then checks its
candidates in list order under each pass (`CandidateWalk`: the pass and
position, the contextual resume point, the explicit-type-argument
recovery and the sole or recovered candidate, by position). A candidate's
check reads its parameters, type parameters, receiver, rest shape and arity
once, as it begins (`CandidatePrologue`). Driven by a frame
(`HelperDrive::Frame`), the walk's candidate list is a request when the
memo does not serve it warm, its delivery read where it was asked. The
runtime produces that read outside the demand scope the walk pushes for
its call site, so the request names the scope it was asked under
(`RequestOrigin`): the frame that produces it keeps that site as a
`DemandScope`, pushes it for each of its steps and hands it to every frame
its needs start, so a primitive or collection callee's list looks its
apparent global up in the call site's project whatever scope lies beneath
the walk. A list whose entry admits its subject (a contextual mapping's
list over a primitive, literal, template, array or tuple) is requested
unentered: the call's frame admits it with the entry's own admission
(`QueryEntryBuild`, held as `AdmissionHolder::Call` beside the call),
under the scope and checking site the walk asked it at; each read the
admission makes is the frame's need, the admitted key is the need last,
and its delivery reaches the walk with the admission's prelude, attributed
where the walk reads it. A set that did not come back as a set is
classified by the callee's list, which is a request too: the slot that
held the set holds the list while the walk waits on it, and its delivery
enters the classification. Before each
first-pass argument relation the operands' first instantiations are
demanded (`OperandDemand`, as for a relation root). One that must be
computed stops the candidate there,
before the relation records anything: the candidate is retained
(`SuspendedCandidate`: its prologue, the arguments it relates, its
collecting session taken out of the transaction, its checkpoint, the next
argument, the failure it met, its SCC deferral and skipped generic-function
arguments, and the pending argument's mapped parameter type, mapping
evidence and demand). The delivery enters that argument's demand; with its
operands answered the relation runs in place over the deliveries, and the
walk continues — no earlier candidate is checked again, the candidate's
prologue is not read again, and no earlier argument relates again. Driven
in place, the walk never stops. A machinery-root call's frame
(`FrameBuild::Call`) drives its walk this way under the executor frame it
opened; `CallRead` drives an inline call's walk the same way under its
executor frame on a consumer's frame, and resolves a machinery-root call
through its family memo in place. The answer-cache re-entry the call frame
used before — re-running the walk from its root over the frame's answers,
skipping to the stopped candidate — is gone.

On a consumer's frame a contextual call's callback round continues that walk
instead of executing the call again. When a candidate's preparation names a
callback, the call keeps its walk (`CallState::Callback` holds the
`CallWalk`: the candidate lists, the acquired arguments and the passes, and
no session); the executor frame closes while the consumer types the
argument, so the callback body never runs under the call's frame. The typed
arguments re-open the frame for the typed call key, after its
answer-at-entry, and the kept walk continues
(`ProjectSemanticDispatch::continue_call_read`): the typed argument is
acquired alone and replaces the plan's arguments authored at its position
(`enter_delivered_argument`), the walk's cursor is derived as a new walk
derives it, so the `resume_at` skip passes over the earlier candidates with
no work, and the suspended candidate's preparation resumes inside the walk
under the executor frame. The callee is not substituted again, the candidate
lists are not read again and no other argument is acquired again. A
callback beside a spread argument or in a generic rest tail holds no
collector, so its candidates are checked again over the kept plan, since
the typed argument changes their inference. The preparation's contextual
mapping reads the signature lists of its parameter types through one read
point (`contextual_signature_nodes`), the same request the walk makes for a
candidate list: a list the memo does not serve warm is the walk's request,
and its delivery is read where it was asked. The mapping keeps what it
computed across the request (`CandidateMapping`: fixation, widening and the
two mappers run once; the resumable contextual type walk keeps a callable
leaf's substituted targets), and the walk records the waiting candidate
(`CandidateWalk::preparing`) and re-enters its preparation with the
delivery. Released while it waits, the call pops its executor frame
undecided and abandons the held collector. Driven in place, a callback
round still resumes through `resume_contextual_call` and executes the call
again, unchanged.

### Frame entry loads

An instantiation's or a `typeof`'s frame on a production poll's drive
performs no load before its build begins. Its first step asks for the
loads that build would perform first and waits on each as an outside input
(`EvalStep::Await`) instead:

- its own file — the instantiation's declaring file, the `typeof`'s value
  file — while it is still loading (`IndexedInputs::begin_indexed_load`);
- then each file that file's prepared declaration bundle routes an import to
  and would load while materializing (`IndexedInputs::begin_prepared_bundle_load`),
  in the order the materialization loads them. The host answers it from the
  bundle's own route-edge plan (`VerterHost::route_dep_edge_plan`, shared
  with `prepared_decl_bundle_route_dep_edges_with_context`), so it begins
  exactly the loads the materialization would perform and never one it
  would not. It materializes nothing and reads the owner only when that
  file is already current.

Nothing of the build has run while the frame waits, so the step the last
load restarts begins the build once, over loaded files. Every file waited on
joins the frame's `awaited_loads` and is never waited on again: a load that
failed supplies its terminal outcome to every read of that file by the
frame. The frame owns that outcome across steps and installs it only while
its build runs; repeated indexed or scope reads cannot submit a synchronous
retry. An independent reader can load the file again after the frame ends.
What the check itself reads is observed in a fact
tracer and observation scope of its own and dropped; the build observes
what it reads. On the synchronous entry's drive the frame waits on nothing,
and its build performs the same loads in place as their external driver.

Behaviour, in the session's `semantic_poll_contract` group: an
instantiation whose declaring file's scheduler source read is held, a
`typeof` whose value file is held, and a `typeof` in a loaded file whose
initializer reads a value imported from a held file each return `Pending`,
stay pending when their `Send` task is polled on another thread, leave the
held file unloaded, and once the read goes on deliver exactly the
synchronous entry's answer, fact status, diagnostics and receipt with as
many instantiations built; an instantiation released while its file loads
leaves no wait, flight or request state open. The WASM control
`semantic_poll_contract_an_instantiation_of_an_unloaded_file_loads_on_the_only_thread`
polls the same instantiation on the driverless single thread and reads what
the synchronous entry serves.

### Cache admission and publication

Both artifact and query-candidate admission use one owned protocol. Artifact
callers enter `singleflight::owned_admit_with_post_publish` or
`owned_admit_with_post_publish_by_flight_key`; query-candidate callers enter
`lookup_publish::owned_admit_with_lookup_publish` in the type engine.

- **Work transfer:** `OwnedAdmission::poll` answers a validated warm value,
  subscribes to a flight, or transfers the sole `OwnedAdmissionProducer`.
  Running that producer is the external owner's job. Polling neither computes
  cold work nor submits to a queue. The producer installs its actual task from
  the waiter's shared registry, and the parent edge remains live until the
  answer is observed or abandoned.
- **Result and failure:** `Value(Some(value))` is complete. `Value(None)` keeps
  the adapter's failure policy. `Cancelled`, `Cycle`, `ProducerUnowned`,
  `ProducerDropped` and `ProducerPanicked` are typed refusals without partial values. The artifact
  follower surfaces failed production; a query follower forks after producer
  panic or rejected admission. Return-only production is never shared across
  views. A cacheable follower revalidates and bubbles facts in its own view.
- **Lifetime:** each subscriber owns one replaceable readiness registration
  and its stop interest. Dropping or cancelling it leaves the producer and
  sibling subscriptions intact. Terminalization releases task pins, its table,
  key and callbacks while a completed handle may remain alive. Dropping an
  unrun producer fails and retires its exact flight and wakes the followers.
- **Publication:** the original validation, retention fences, pointer-checked
  retirement, displaced-entry cleanup and deferred eviction stay in the
  owning adapter. Wakes occur after publication locks release. The same-key
  and split-flight artifact entries preserve their different publish locking.
- **Compatibility:** synchronous admission/get-or-insert entries externally
  drive this same state machine with borrowed callbacks. Explicit owned APIs
  require owned transferable views/tracers; they refuse a pending producer
  whose shared task authority is unavailable with `ProducerUnowned`. A ready
  answer needs no wait authority. No extra registry or pool is
  allocated by standalone compatibility calls.

The dispatch layer owns conversion of `cache_runtime::node::{lookup,
query::lookup}` and their semantic memo consumers. Metadata and compilation
callers reach those nodes through dispatch and retain responsibility for their
own resumable traversal state.

### Workspace resolution

`WorkspaceRead::begin_resolution(Arc<Self>, ResolutionRequest, waiter, stop)`
returns `WorkspaceResolution`. Importer, specifier, context, optional published
root, immutable overlay and explicit project are owned inputs. The separate
`begin_project_resolution` accepts a project-only request without inventing an
importer. Engine-backed memory and filesystem workspaces implement both;
adapters without that ownership protocol return `ResolutionError::Unsupported`.

The subscriber polls `ResolutionStep::{Produce,Value}`. The independent
`ResolutionProducer` only transfers `ResolutionWork` or registers readiness;
only external `ResolutionWork::run` advances the retained continuation. Work
uses the caller's existing execution service, creates no pool or CPU authority,
and installs its producer task for that slice. Parser state remains in the
existing resolver owner; only owned resolution records cross this interface.

Phases include stable-world capture, candidate validation, bounded input
preflight and loading, filesystem discovery, freezing, replay and publication.
Flight and publication-gate contention register wakes and shared-registry wait
edges instead of parking the owner execution. Input loading transfers external
work before performing IO. An incomplete input load, gate or flight can be held
while other already-admitted work continues; resuming never admits a new root
or discards the captured attempt. Discovery and replay share the same
`InputResolutionLedger`, including attempts, bytes, churn, staged inputs and
completed-output ownership. Directory evidence is owned by that continuation
and installed only for external slices, so migration cannot lose it in worker
TLS. Frozen-reader completeness and independent live validation still govern
filesystem admission.

The host injects its TaskRegistry through `WorkspaceServices::attach`, including
workspace replacement. If a workspace is shared by hosts, attach joins their
existing cycle graphs through `TaskRegistry::share_authority_with`; it never
revokes earlier tasks or creates another task service. Generation-qualified task
ids retain their registration namespace, live wait edges survive composition,
and installed scopes follow the joined authority. Union by rank and ordered
root locks make concurrent joins safe. The last registration-owner release
removes its pool, so repeated workspace swaps retain no pool history.
Standalone workspace compatibility calls use their own composition authority. Flights own producer leases across threads; every joining
task has its own subscription and wait edge. Same-path and mixed scheduler/flight
cycles return `Cycle`, including a nested request on the same OS thread. A shared
candidate is adopted only after validation in the subscriber's captured world;
retention refusal still delivers the complete unretained answer. The final fence
also rejects a replaced pinned published root.

Cancellation wakes and detaches only the subscriber. Transferred producer work
continues for other demands. Its external owner must finish it or drop it; dropping
a producer or transferred slice publishes `ProducerDropped`, and an unwind
publishes `ProducerPanicked`. An abandoned inner candidate flight wakes followers
for their own bounded retry. Explicit request cancellation (including a host
reset/shutdown stop token) returns `Cancelled`; a changed pinned world returns
the existing noncacheable `ResolutionViewSuperseded` outcome. No stop admits a
partial answer. Terminal observation/drop releases exactly that subscriber's
registrations, wait edges and task pins. Flight retirement removes only its exact
lane; all notifications run after the table/slot/publication locks release.

The synchronous importer and project entries externally drive these same
continuations. Legacy boolean cancellation is sampled only at that external
compatibility driver; token-owned production entries register stop wakes. Polling
capabilities expose neither that driver nor the underlying engine/reader.
Completion/flight replies exert no bounded-queue backpressure; input reservations
retain their existing explicit budget and integrity outcomes. Observation remains
the existing optional VFS/audit capture, with no new history or enabled checks.

Above this handoff, host loading and index dependency extraction
(`host_lifecycle::resolve_import_outcome_in`, `host_executor::extract_deps`),
resolver import/route demands, explicit-project intrinsics and program ordering
belong to DISPATCH; metadata import traversal belongs to META; hydration and
compile traversal above program ordering belong to COMPILE. Those caller
conversions consume these APIs separately from the dependency owner. A
compile root's blocker hydration and dependency verdict's walk consume
them through `VerterHost::resolve_for_persistent_state_pollable`, which
takes the same route, records the same owner edge and admits with the same
witness as the synchronous `resolve_for_persistent_state` and hands its
driver the resolution's producer.

## What a pollable root still needs

The cold component-metadata root resolves macros through
`ProjectSemanticDispatch`, extracts the analysis (which recurses into
imported components' fallthrough), and publishes through
`ComponentMetaResultPublish`, all synchronously inside the poll that follows
its capture; the compile root's handed-out macro producer runs the same
kind of traversal. Every wait in the table above can be reached from inside
that nested synchronous call graph — the measured fixture alone reaches
fourteen, up to 138 frames deep. A root becomes fully pollable only when
each such call either answers without waiting or suspends as owned state,
and resumption continues the suspended computation rather than re-running
it. Converting the low-level waits alone does not achieve this; the callers
that reach them have to become resumable too.

The conversion that remains runs from each wait upward to the root, one
caller at a time, as compiler-generated continuation state (`async` bodies
polled by the root), so a suspended call keeps its locals, its traversal
position and its completed results, and resuming it repeats none of them.
What is already in place for that:

- the roots themselves, their ambient installation, their task identity
  and the `RootWork` handoff;
- a `Sync` dispatcher, so continuation state that borrows it is `Send`;
- task-owned resolver flights whose leader may suspend inside its compute,
  and workspace leases that cannot cross a suspension;
- pollable forms of every leaf wait in the table;
- the component-meta root's cold path down to its input capture, with the
  scopes it holds carried per poll
  ([Scopes a suspended compute carries](#scopes-a-suspended-compute-carries)).

What stays synchronous inside a cold component-meta poll: the request
host's `compute_component_meta` (macro resolution through
`ProjectSemanticDispatch`), `extract_component_meta_from_resolved`
(including fallthrough into imported components), and output
materialization. The next callers to convert are
`compute_component_meta` and the semantic task drive it reaches, under the
same carries.

Each scope a converted caller holds across a suspension point needs the
per-poll carry the scopes the component-meta path already holds use
([Scopes a suspended compute carries](#scopes-a-suspended-compute-carries)):
the scope's state is owned by the suspended computation and installed for
each poll on the polling thread. Where a
scope's guard is thread-bound — the spend log, a workspace lease, a borrow of
the dispatcher's state — the compiler rejects holding it across a
suspension; a scope whose guard is `Send` has to be given its carry
explicitly before a caller that holds it is converted.
